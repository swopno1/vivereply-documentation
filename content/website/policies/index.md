# Policies — Staff Training Module

**Pages:** `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/refund-policy`.
**Audience:** managers, front desk.
**Time to complete:** about 10 minutes.
**Prerequisite:** the [Website](..) module.

## Learning objectives

After this module you know what each policy page promises and when to
read it to a guest.

## 1. The pages

| Page | What it covers |
|---|---|
| `/terms` | Booking terms: rates, check-in/out times, house rules, liability |
| `/privacy` | What guest data the property collects and how it's used — names, contacts, stay history, and the [guest record](../../guests/) discipline behind it |
| `/refund-policy` | When money comes back: cancellation windows, refund timelines, how refunds are processed |

## 2. When they matter

- **At booking disputes:** "I didn't know check-out was 11" — the terms
  were shown at booking. Read them *with* the guest, don't quote them
  *at* the guest.
- **At cancellation:** the refund policy is the script. Follow it exactly
  — the [refund flow](../../bookings/detail/) in the booking detail
  implements it. If the policy and the guest's expectation differ,
  escalate to the manager; don't improvise.
- **At data questions:** "what do you keep about me?" — the privacy page
  is the answer, and the [guest privacy discipline](../../guests/) is how
  the property honors it.

## 3. Discipline

Policy pages are **legal promises**. Changing them is an owner task, and
changes apply **going forward** — a booking made under the old refund
policy is governed by the old refund policy. Never edit a policy page to
win an argument with a guest in front of you.

## 4. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Check first |
|---|---|
| Guest disputes a term | Read the page together — what does it actually say? |
| Policy contradicts practice | Practice is wrong until the policy is deliberately changed by the owner. |
| Outdated policy | Flag it to the owner — don't freelance the fix. |
